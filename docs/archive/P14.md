### 1.6 Next big piece (after the 26 Oct patch): the crew take, measured first

What every economy piece above says together [measured]: the price and the model now allow big takes (R4-1 a) and the
gate logic sees every camp (R4-3), yet golems are never taken before r300 and monoliths almost never. R4: the winners'
gem gap is entirely big monsters, taken by 3–4 hitters together (K) or 2 strong ones (P). Our bodies spread, and a
group is priced per body (value / k over the take's rounds plus the partners' wait). So the next piece is team-level,
not a constant.

**Measure before building (a TRACE copy on the sprint 2 golem maps and the pool's golem / monolith maps):**
1. Is the big group a candidate at all? `near6` lists the 6 nearest groups by walk; a golem 7th never gets priced.
2. `need_k_r9`'s k for it with the claimers present (0 = never takeable from my HP).
3. Its price against the chosen one (the `choice` trace line has the best and second).
4. How many allies are within 10 when it is the best price, and how long the partner wait runs before `WAIT_CAP`
   fails it.

**Design sketch (to settle on the measures):** a crew call. A body whose best price is a group with k ≥ 3 claims it as
the team's take (its slot byte, `C_FARM` + the group); every ally within reach prices that group with the claim
counted (`CL.cl`), the value not split below k bodies, and the walk to the rally point instead of a per-body wait; the
call holds until the take or a timeout; the recall-and-return session (`P14_R4-1c_sessions.diff`) rides on top once
the crew is there. Judged on the sprint 2 maps (golem stratum) and the pool; measures: golem / monolith gems a game,
need > roster gems, bodies within 5 at the first hit, deaths to camps.

**A comms limit found while reading `comms.cpp`:** a claim names a group by its draft index in 5 bits (`mi`), so only
the first 31 groups can be claimed or announced as captured (`mi` 31 = unclaimable). The ring map and other sprint 2
maps have 35 groups. Comms v3 (2.4) must address groups by something that doesn't run out (e.g. the claim's group
chosen among the nearest 32 to a shared anchor, or two bytes for the team's crew call).
